Method & sources

How the map was built, and where each claim comes from.

AI-generated. The research, coding and scoring behind this map were produced with AI and then reviewed. Be aware it may still contain erros.

Structure. Three levels: 12 domains (the underlying human need), 43 sub-domains, and individual use cases. Domains are arranged in a ring so neighbours share themes (Learn sits next to Science, Mind next to Spirit, Play next to Create). Dashed links mark cases that belong to more than one domain.

Each use case is coded on: field · problem type (what's wrong) · purpose (what the person wants) · AI role (the job AI does) · mode (augment = person stays in the loop, automate = AI does it, relate = the relationship is the point) · how common (1–5) · how original (1–5).

Scores are editorial. "How common" reflects published usage studies where they exist (OpenAI/NBER, Anthropic Economic Index, HBR Top-100) and reporting otherwise. "How original" is distance from mainstream expectations of a chatbot. Treat both as ranks, not measurements.

Evidence. Every use case links to at least one source showing that people actually do it: a usage study, survey, product figure or news report. Open a use case to see its sources. Links were checked in November 2026.

Scope. Consumer chat use, professional use, and specialised AI systems (science, agriculture, accessibility). Harmful uses (scams, deepfakes) appear only where they create a counter-use.
